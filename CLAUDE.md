# CLAUDE.md — authoring and continuation guide

This file is binding for every session that writes or edits this book, human or agent. Read it in
full, then read `PROJECT_STATUS.md`, before changing anything.

## 1. Starting a session

1. Read `PROJECT_STATUS.md`: what is done, what is in flight, the next tasks.
2. Run `.venv/bin/python tools/validate_book.py` (create the venv with
   `uv venv --python 3.12 .venv && uv pip install --python .venv/bin/python -r <(echo mkdocs mkdocs-material pyyaml ruff)`
   or `pip install mkdocs mkdocs-material pyyaml` if it is missing). Fix any failure before new work.
3. Pick the highest-priority task from the status file. Do not start a chapter whose sources are
   not yet in `research/bibliography.yaml` with verified fields.
4. At the end of the session, update `PROJECT_STATUS.md` (completed artifacts, validation result,
   open research issues, next concrete tasks). Never mark work done that is not.

## 3. Publication status

A chapter (or template, or project) is in one of four states in `curriculum.yaml`:

- `planned`: designed, not written. Not in the nav.
- `draft`: file exists, not yet passing validation. Not in the nav.
- `review`: passes `validate_book.py` and an editorial self-review against the human review
  checklist. In the nav, and must carry the banner `!!! warning "Awaiting human review"` directly
  under the H1 (enforced). This is the highest status an agent may set.
- `published`: additionally passed the human review checklist
  (`research/human-review-checklist.md`) done by a person, recorded in `research/quality-report.md`.
  The banner is removed (enforced). Only a human reviewer moves a chapter to `published`.

Moving to `review` means: set the status, add the chapter and its solutions page to the nav in
`mkdocs.yml`, add the banner, regenerate, validate. A skeleton must never reach the nav.

## 4. The chapter contract

Every chapter file starts with `# Chapter N — Title` (title exactly as in `curriculum.yaml`, em
dash with spaces) and an opening paragraph, then has exactly these H2 sections, in this order:

1. `## Why This Matters`: why the topic matters at team, organization, and executive scale.
2. `## Learning Objectives`: three to six bullets, each a capability ("Diagnose…", "Build…").
3. `## The Mental Model`: the one or two ideas that organize the rest. Open with a blockquote
   `> **Mental model:** …`.
4. `## Deep Conceptual Explanation`: the rigorous treatment, with research, `###` subsections.
5. `## Engineering Organization Example`: a realistic application, worked with numbers.
6. `## Decision Framework`: criteria for choosing among approaches; usually a table or a decision diagram.
7. `## Trade-offs`: costs, constraints, alternatives.
8. `## Failure Modes`: named failures, what they look like, how to detect them early.
9. `## Enterprise Application`: the topic in an organization of 50–150 engineers.
10. `## CTO Perspective`: how the topic changes at executive scale.
11. `## Practical Playbook`: numbered, actionable steps.
12. `## Case Study`: Northstar, or a real case from a cited public source.
13. `## Exercises`: three `###` levels, exactly `### Foundational`, `### Applied Leadership`,
    `### Executive Decision-Making`. Exercises are bold-numbered paragraphs `**F1.**`, `**A1.**`,
    `**X1.**`. At least 2 F, 2 A, 1 X. Each sets up a situation (40+ words). **No answers here.**
14. `## Reflection Questions`: questions that challenge the reader's assumptions; no answers.
15. `## Key Takeaways`: six to ten operational principles.
16. `## References and Further Reading`: cited sources with a line on what each is good for.

Other rules enforced by `validate_book.py`: at least 2,500 words, at least two Mermaid diagrams,
no section under 60 words, no placeholder text. Aim for 2,500–5,000 meaningful words; depth and
accuracy beat length. Do not pad.

Solutions: `docs/solutions/chNN-solutions.md`, one `## F1 — short title` section per exercise
(120+ words each). Show the reasoning, state the criteria a good answer meets, and name the common
weak answer. Where several answers are defensible, say so.

## 5. Research and citation rules

- Cite by linking to the bibliography anchor: `[Forsgren et al., 2018](../../references/bibliography.md#forsgren2018accelerate)`.
  `check_references.py` resolves every such link and refuses ids that are missing or unverified.
- A published chapter cites at least five distinct sources, and not only practitioner literature.
- Never invent a quotation, page number, statistic, finding, study, URL, or DOI. If it cannot be
  confirmed on a page fetched in the session, it does not go in.
- Respect `access_level`. A `metadata-only` source may be credited for the idea it is known for, in
  the book's words, with no quotation, page number, or reported finding. A finding needs a source
  that was read at least at abstract or official-summary level, and the claim must stay within
  what that level supports.
- Label evidence strength in the prose, using the vocabulary in `docs/references/evidence-labels.md`:
  research finding, industry research, standard, practitioner consensus, expert opinion,
  this book's recommendation, illustrative.
- New sources: add them to `research/bibliography.yaml` with `verified_fields`, `verified_via`,
  `verified_on`, `access_level`, and conservative `claims`. If verification fails, record the
  limitation and do not cite.
- Contested topics are written up in `research/disputed-claims.md` before a chapter takes a side.
- Thin evidence goes in `research/research-gaps.md` and is admitted in the chapter.
- The Google SRE books and *Software Engineering at Google* are CC BY-NC-ND. Summarize and cite;
  never copy or adapt their text or figures.

## 6. Northstar Technologies

The canonical description is `docs/case-studies/northstar/index.md` (company, people, system),
`baseline-data.md` (the measured starting state, including the extended baseline by topic), and
`first-quarter.md` (the director's first 90 days: decisions, timeline, day-90 results), with data
files in `docs/case-studies/northstar/data/`. The CSV files win any disagreement. Use those names
and numbers exactly. If a chapter needs a new fact, add it to the canonical pages first, check it
against the existing data (recompute any aggregate it affects), then use it. Every Northstar passage
must be recognizably fictional. Never put a real company into an invented story.

## 6a. Parallel authoring

When several authors write chapters at once, none of them edits a shared file. Each writes only its
chapter, its solutions file, and three side files that the maintainer merges afterwards:
`research/pending-sources/chNN.yaml` (new sources; `tools/booklib.py` loads these alongside the
bibliography so citations validate), `research/northstar-additions/chNN.md` (proposed canon facts),
and `research/evidence-matrix-chNN.md`. An author validates a chapter before it is in the nav with
`ELH_TREAT_AS_LIVE=NN python tools/validate_book.py`, ignoring nav, stale-page, and missing-anchor
errors. The maintainer then merges sources into `bibliography.yaml`, facts into the Northstar canon
(resolving conflicts between authors), matrices into `evidence-matrix.md`, sets status `review`,
adds nav entries, regenerates, and validates.

## 7. Voice

- Reader: a strong software engineer or engineering leader; no formal management, finance, or
  psychology training assumed. Explain those terms once, briefly.
- Direct, concrete, precise. Short paragraphs. American English.
- No motivational filler, no "in today's fast-paced world", no generic "leadership is important".
- Be opinionated where evidence is strong; always state trade-offs, context, and alternatives.
- Every metric comes with how it is gamed. Every organizational change comes with its transition
  cost. Every framework comes with how it fails.
- Quantitative examples state their assumptions.
- Em dashes only in chapter titles. In prose use commas, colons, parentheses, or new sentences.

## 8. Diagrams

Fenced ```mermaid blocks. Each diagram shows a mechanism (a loop, a flow, a decision, a structure),
not decoration. Diagrams must be readable at phone width (390 px): use `flowchart TD`, not `LR`,
for anything longer than three nodes in a row; keep node labels to about four words; no level with
more than four nodes side by side. Put explanations in a table or prose under the diagram, not in the
nodes. Quote any label containing parentheses, semicolons, or colons:
`A["Lead time (days)"]`. `validate_book.py` lints; `tools/render_diagrams.py` renders with
mermaid-cli in CI.
