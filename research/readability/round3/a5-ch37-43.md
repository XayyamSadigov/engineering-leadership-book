### P1 (high) docs/parts/08-management-operating-system/39-reviews-decision-logs-escalation-and-executive-reporting.md:547 — F1 depends on an exemption the chapter never introduces

For example: "DL-11, week 9: auth-svc exempt from the shared pipeline template until end of quarter 2; renewal decided by the domain staff engineer; exemptions last at most two quarters." Or replace the F1 item with an exemption the chapter already contains, such as core/permissions in DL-04.

## Patterns across your scope
- **Northstar recaps and retellings across chapters**: Week 7 severity audit, INC-427 slip, ADR-0001, core/permissions, and the bulk-ordering date recur in 37, 39, 40, 41, and 43. Each chapter re-explains some and assumes others. Recommended book-wide rule: each episode has one "home" chapter where it is told in full. Elsewhere it gets one clause plus a link, never a retelling, and never a bare reference with no reminder.
- **Glossary entries exist but are not linked**: customer-detected share, calibration, stay interview, Core. Recommended rule: run a lint that flags a glossary headword appearing in a chapter without a link at its first occurrence.
- **Numbers in prose after a table**: Ch 38:286, Ch 38:411 (Ch 42 case), Ch 40:236, Ch 41:210-213, Ch 42:411, Ch 43:398-410 and 419-425. The table does the work, then a paragraph re-derives or extends it in three units. Recommended rule: any paragraph with more than three figures becomes a table row or moves to the solutions.
- **Cross-chapter calendar and capacity reconciliation**: Ch 37 ↔ Ch 40 (director hours), Ch 38 ↔ Ch 42 (463 engineer-weeks). These are correct but costly to read. Recommended rule: a later chapter quotes the earlier figure it needs once, with a link, and does not reconcile the bookkeeping in the text.
- **Exercises that need facts outside the chapter**: Ch 39 F1 (auth-svc exemption), Ch 40 X2 (CX structure, travel and tooling budget), Ch 41 A3 (Bram). Recommended rule: every named fact an exercise relies on must appear in the chapter, or the exercise must name the page that has it.
- **Strong, consistent chapter skeleton**: Every chapter has a stated mental model, a diagram that shows a mechanism, a decision table, named failure modes with signals, and a fictional dilemma left open for the reader. That consistency makes the part easy to navigate. Keep it.

## Missing or thin content
- **Hiring and onboarding engineering managers** (Ch 41): The bench covers only internal growth. There is nothing on hiring a manager from outside (what to assess, how the rubric maps to an interview loop) or on a new manager's first 90 days beyond "gets the rubric on day one". This belongs in Ch 41 Enterprise Application, about 300–400 words, or as a pointer if another chapter owns hiring.
- **Same-day escalation triggers** (Ch 39): See P9. About 80 words.
- **Measuring the growth allowance** (Ch 38): The chapter makes the growth allowance the most sensitive input (the F2 solution says so), but says only "from your own history". It needs a two-to-three-sentence method: ratio of delivered to estimated effort over the last N completed items, per team, with the median and the spread. About 80 words in the Terms box or Practical Playbook.
- **Running the decision meeting itself** (Ch 37): The five design choices cover inputs and outputs. They say little about facilitation in the room: handling a decider who is absent, timeboxing an item, or what to do when the meeting cannot converge (pointing to Ch 39's escalation would do). About 100–150 words.
- **Managing up to the CTO** (Ch 40): The CTO Perspective covers the upward account, but the director's own relationship with their manager (agreeing expectations, the report contract, disagreement) is spread across Ch 39 and Ch 42. A short subsection in Ch 40 that points to both would help, about 150 words.
