## Top problems (most impact on the reader first, at most 15)

### P1 (high) docs/parts/15-capstone/60-capstone-northstar.md:168 — "Stage" means two different things
- What the reader experiences: the whole chapter numbers its capstone stages 1–10. The prevention table then says the continuation reaches "at most 18% by stage 3", and F3 (line 627) speaks of "Amara Okafor's condition that the allocation be fixed per stage". Those mean the continuation's own stages ("Hold", "Converge", defined only in the quarter 4 briefing), not capstone stage 3. A careful reader goes looking for the link to the scorecard stage and finds none.
- Example: "| Week 45 | Continuation proposal | 6 FTE in quarters 5–6, 5 in quarters 7–8 | at most 18% by stage 3 |"
- Fix: in Chapter 60 (chapter text and solutions), never use "stage" for the continuation. Write "by quarter 8 (the continuation's Converge phase)" and "fixed per continuation phase". Or rename the continuation's stages "phases" across the book.

Move the seat-option table to the X1 exercise or the solutions, and say only "the open decisions put the engineer count anywhere from 77 to 80". Cut the term table to the three terms the section actually uses.

## Patterns across your scope
- **Inside-baseball week numbers and earlier-chapter references:** Chapter 59's case table (weeks 7, 9, 10, 11, 39) and Chapter 60 throughout (weeks 4, 14, 19, 26, 39, 41, 44, 46, 47, 48, 49, 60, 78, 104). Most come with a few words of context, but the density is high. Book-wide rule: at most one week number per sentence, and in the Engineering Organization Example only the weeks the lesson needs. Replace the others with phrases like "early in the year" or "in quarter 3".
- **The Halvard comparison company is used before its rules are introduced:** "six enterprise policies", "tier 1 minimums", and "the estimate rule" appear in Chapters 56 and 58 before Chapter 59 lists them. Book-wide rule: the first Halvard passage in each chapter gets a short "Halvard so far" box, mirroring "Northstar so far", with a link to the Halvard page's policy list.
- **Number piles in Enterprise Application and Engineering Organization Example sections:** worst in Chapter 59 (about 12 tables), then Chapter 60 (about 14 tables in total) and the Halvard outage in Chapter 57. Book-wide rule: one table per point. Move secondary arithmetic (spans, in-year gaps, binomial chances) to solutions. State the conclusion in prose and the arithmetic in a table, never both.
- **Retelling the same Northstar episode:** Engineering Excellence Day is retold in Chapter 57 (resistance), Chapter 59 (an addition that bought nothing), and indirectly in Chapter 60. The capacity-versus-credits "self-funding" argument appears in Chapters 59 and 60, though Chapter 60 sensibly links back. Book-wide rule: one owning chapter tells the episode (Chapter 43 for EED). Others use one sentence and a link.
- **Diagram vocabulary drifts from table vocabulary:** Chapter 59's two diagrams and its verdict table use three label sets. Chapter 60's flow ("Revise, measurement bridge") roughly matches its status list (honored, bridged, revised in writing, retired). Book-wide rule: decision-diagram end nodes use exactly the verdict names in the accompanying table.
- **Solutions that use facts the chapter does not give:** Chapter 56 A1, and Chapter 60 A3 and F2. Book-wide rule: any fact a solution relies on is either in the exercise prompt or one link away in it.

## Missing or thin content
- **Merger of peers (Chapter 58):** the introduction promises the merger of peers, but it gets one paragraph (lines 256–260) with no example. Add three or four sentences on choosing the surviving platform and process when neither side is the acquirer, or a short Halvard vignette, in Deep Conceptual Explanation. About 150 words.
- **Severance and support sizing (Chapter 58):** severance is called "the main lever on the people staying", but nothing says what a reasonable package or outplacement support looks like, or how to choose between options. Add a short paragraph with ranges framed as practitioner consensus, under Fairness and ethics. About 120 words.
- **Internal communication during a crisis (Chapter 57):** "internal messaging" is a leadership-track duty, but there is no guidance on what to tell staff during and after a public failure (what non-responders should say to customers, how to stop speculation in internal channels). Add two or three bullets to the external-communication-by-phase table or the Practical Playbook. About 100 words.
- **A filled commitment register row (Chapter 60):** the register is the capstone's central tool, but the chapter never shows a filled example row. The solutions do, in the sample stage 1. Add a three-row example (severity definitions, tier 1 list, core/permissions exemption) after "Before you start". About 80 words plus a table.
- **Running the inventory in practice (Chapter 59):** the playbook says to inventory every standing claim, but not how long it takes, who does it, or how to price a forum quickly (attendees × hours × 46 weeks, say). One paragraph with a pricing rule of thumb. About 100 words.

## What works well (keep)
- Every chapter opens with a genuine, one-paragraph mental model, often with a loop diagram (Chapter 56's three loops and Chapter 57's candor loop are especially clear). The later sections really do build on it.
- Chapter 57's Halvard Checkout outage is a model worked example. The timeline, the true-but-misleading macro, the correction text, and three priced compensation options make every principle concrete.
- Chapter 58's "decide the work before the people", with its step table of "what goes wrong when it is skipped", plus the ownership check and the manager's first-two-minutes script: concrete, humane, and actionable.
- Chapter 59's four-question load test and its "the pack's load had been the meeting, not the document" failure story. Showing a removal that went wrong is rare and valuable.
- Evidence labels are used consistently and honestly ("research finding, single case", "the transfer to software engineers is this book's inference").
- Hypothetical scenarios in the exercises are clearly marked as not part of the record, and the sampled solutions (Chapter 57 F1, Chapter 58 X1, Chapter 59 F2 and F3, Chapter 60 F3 and A3) give criteria and name the weak answer.
- Chapter 60's "Judging earlier stages without outcome bias" and its hinge questions are a distinctive, transferable idea, and the short path (45–55 hours) makes the capstone attainable.
