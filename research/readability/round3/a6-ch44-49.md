## Top problems (most impact on the reader first, at most 15)

### P1 (high) docs/parts/10-business-leadership/45-business-models-and-engineering-economics.md:160 — Three rates for engineer time in one paragraph
- What the reader experiences: the paragraph is meant to settle the price of engineer time once. Instead it gives the reader $3,558 per week, $46,250 per FTE-quarter, $800 per day, and $157,250 per capitalized FTE-year, and explains why they differ. A reader with no finance training finishes the paragraph unsure which number to use. "Capitalized" is not explained here at all; it is deferred to Chapter 52.
- Example: "A few chapters quote **Finance's day rate** of $800 per engineer-day ($185,000 ÷ about 230 working days); it is higher per day because Finance counts only working days. $157,250 per capitalized FTE-year is the capitalized share (85 percent) of $185,000 (Chapter 52)."
- Fix: keep one sentence that gives the planning rate ($185,000 a year, which is $3,558 a week and $46,250 a quarter). Move the day rate and the capitalized figure to a glossary entry, or to a footnote-style admonition titled "Other rates you will see", with a link to Chapter 52.

### P2 (high) docs/parts/10-business-leadership/45-business-models-and-engineering-economics.md:333 — Arithmetic in prose in the key "what the table says" step and in the case
- What the reader experiences: Step 3 is where the chapter's lesson lands: fragility matters more than the ranking. The bullets mix that lesson with inline calculations ("0.15 × $1.17M × 72% = $126,000"), so the reader has to compute and absorb at the same time. The case study repeats the pattern at lines 485–491.
- Example: "If it cut by 15 points the probability of losing one top-40 customer, that alone adds about 0.15 × $1.17M × 72% = $126,000 a year, and B passes A."
- Fix: give each bullet its conclusion only ("Counting renewal protection could put B ahead of A"). Put the sensitivity figures in one small table (input, value tested, effect on NPV), or move them to the solutions. The book's own voice rule already asks for arithmetic in tables or solutions.

## Patterns across your scope
- **Arithmetic in prose:** Ch 45 (Step 3, case note), Ch 49 (cost paragraphs at 278–281 and page one), Ch 46 (option C). Book-wide rule: a sentence carries the conclusion, and a table or the solutions carry the calculation. No sentence should contain more than one multiplication.
- **Inside-baseball case detail:** Ch 45 (slot mechanics), Ch 47 (alternative D), Ch 48 (Core framework migration, CI consolidation phase 1, ledger-export contract), Ch 49 (notification-svc tier change, ADR-0001, four build systems). Rule: every case fact must feed a decision the reader is asked to make. Otherwise cut it, or give it a one-clause reminder at first use.
- **Facts needed for exercises sit only in the solutions:** Ch 48 (option derivations, departures by team, the 1.4 loading factor). Rule: a figure the reader is asked to weigh must have its basis stated in the chapter, even in one line.
- **Cash versus capacity is re-taught:** Ch 45 (lines 168–173), Ch 48 (439–441), Ch 49 (278–281). The repetition is useful, but each later chapter should name the idea and link to Ch 45's section rather than re-derive it.
- **Source-access hedges ("the abstract reports", "is known for"):** Ch 45, 46, 48, 49. Rule: claim plus evidence label, with no access notes in reader text.
- **Dense research paragraphs:** Ch 47 and Ch 48 stack three or four sources per paragraph, each with a label. Rule: lead each subsection with the rule the reader should apply, then the evidence, one source per sentence.
- **"Northstar so far" boxes:** consistent and helpful. They repeat the same Sev1/Sev2 line in every chapter, which is fine, but the box should also carry the one fact the chapter's case depends on (for example, Ch 47 should explain alternative D).

## Missing or thin content
- **Ch 45, unit economics:** "business models" covers ARR, churn, and margin, but not customer acquisition cost, lifetime value, or net revenue retention, which an engineer meeting a CFO will hear. Add a short paragraph and two rows to the five-question table (about 150 words).
- **Ch 45, internal rate of return:** finance partners often quote IRR. One sentence defining it and saying why NPV is preferred for comparing options would prevent confusion (about 60 words).
- **Ch 48, layers:** see P11. A worked instance is needed (about 150 words), or the topic should move to Ch 34.
- **Ch 48, workforce cost drift beyond level mix:** merit raises and market adjustments raise cost at flat headcount and flat mix. One paragraph in "What a workforce plan contains" (about 100 words).
- **Ch 46, a full five-part no as dialogue:** only part 1 has an example sentence. A four-to-six-line sample reply in the deep section would anchor F3 and A1 (about 80 words).
- **Ch 47, the VP's peers:** the chapter covers directors and the CTO well but says little about the VP's working relationship with product and finance peers, the counterpart to Ch 44's contract one level up (about 150 words in Enterprise or CTO Perspective).

## What works well (keep)
- Ch 44's cause table (lines 270–276), where an engineering finding changes each bet. It makes "information that changes the option set" concrete. The date-origin table is equally clear.
- Ch 45's discounting section (179–219): builds from first principles with a small table and a worked NPV. It is the best finance teaching in the scope.
- Ch 46's mental model, the decision diagram for requests, and the Poisson table with its plain-language reading ("misses 'below 10' in about two quarters of five").
- Ch 47's role-by-decision table, the gap-by-area tables, and "what the org chart would have done". Together they make partition dependence visible in numbers.
- Ch 48's two level-mix policy tables, which show senior drift without any prose arithmetic, and the four-question backfill table.
- Ch 49's sample page one, the objection-sorting table, and the three constructive-politics tests (transcript, record, interests). These are immediately usable.
- The sampled solutions (44 A1, 45 X1, 47 X1, 48 A1 and X1, 49 F3) show their reasoning, give criteria, and name the weak answer. Except for Ch 48 X1's reliance on facts that appear only in the solutions, the exercises can be solved from the chapters.
