## Top problems (most impact on the reader first, at most 15)

### P1 (high) all ten chapters (e.g. 04-the-leadership-ladder.md:434, 06-feedback-conflict-and-difficult-conversations.md:531) — No chapter links the cast page, yet each one adds named people
- What the reader experiences: Chapter 1's case brings in nine managers and staff engineers in one table (01:441–462). After that every chapter adds new names (Rafael, Aisha, Hannah, Mateo, Teodora, Tamsin, Sunil, Yusuf, Bram, Tomasz), and exercises bring in executives with only a role tag: Greg Tanaka (04:434), Helena Marsh (03:490, 04:473), Amara Okafor (06:531), Rajiv Menon. A reader who opens Chapter 6 or 9 directly cannot tell who is who or how these people relate. `cast.md` exists and solves this, but none of the ten chapters links it (grep: 0 links). The glossary and timeline are linked.
- Example: "Two quarters after the director's arrival, Amara Okafor (CPO) and Victor Lindqvist (CTO) disagree in the executive meeting…" (06:531)
- Fix: add "Who's who: [cast](../../case-studies/northstar/cast.md)" to every "Northstar so far" box and to every Exercises preamble, next to the baseline link. At each person's first mention in a chapter, give the role in a few words (Chapter 4 and 8 already do this for managers; do the same for executives in exercises).

### P2 (high) 01-what-engineering-management-is.md:501 — Exercise F3 cannot be solved from the chapter
- What the reader experiences: F3 asks "where a figure like 75 percent might have come from." The solution's answer is a 1.75× boss-versus-worker productivity figure in the Lazear abstract (ch01-solutions F3). The chapter never mentions it, so a reader cannot get there from the text.

### P3 (medium-high) 10-motivation-retention-and-senior-engineers.md:71–86 — Turnover research is cited, but the reader learns nothing from it
- What the reader experiences: the two meta-analyses are summed up as "what predicts turnover depends on the setting", with no predictor named. Lee and Mitchell's model is "four distinct decision paths", and the four are never described. F1 and F2 then ask the reader to write exit questions that "capture… decision paths" and to map interview questions to the model. A reader who has never met the unfolding model has only the label.
- Example: "Lee and Mitchell's unfolding model describes voluntary turnover as four distinct decision paths, each with its own psychological process and external events…"
- Fix: give the four paths in a four-row table, one line each (for example: a shock with a pre-existing plan; a shock that triggers leaving without a search; a shock that triggers a search and comparison; slow dissatisfaction, then a search). Stay within what the bibliography's access level supports. Name two or three predictors that the meta-analyses found to be reasonably consistent, or say plainly that the chapter will not list them and why.

### P4 (medium) 07-hiring-and-structured-interviews.md:70–118 — Validity statistics arrive long before their meaning

The payoff, "what a validity of .42 buys" (hire success goes from 50 to about 64 percent), comes 170 lines later at 07:289, in the Engineering Organization Example. A target reader stalls on the table before learning why the number matters. The arcsin method note (07:297) adds weight for little reader value.
- Example: "the 80 percent credibility interval is the range within which the true validity falls in 80 percent of settings"
- Fix: move the 50 → 64 percent translation, with its 56 and 73 percent edges, to directly after the table (07:111). Keep the cost arithmetic in the example. Move the arcsin formula to a footnote or the solutions. In the table, keep only the structured-interview row and the "about .20 lower" deltas.

### P5 (medium) 06-feedback-conflict-and-difficult-conversations.md:141–173 — The conflict-research passage is dense and out of order

## Patterns across your scope
- **Case material shows up in the Example section before the Case Study introduces it** (Ch 5, 9, 10; Teodora in Ch 6). Book-wide rule: an Engineering Organization Example is either self-contained or opens with a one-line pointer to the Case Study. The Case Study never retells what the Example already showed.
- **The same idea is re-taught across chapters.** Manager-effect evidence appears in Ch 1, 2, 4, and 10. The Peter Principle and Benson are explained in Ch 2, 4, and 8. The mandate-less staff engineer is a failure mode in Ch 1, 4, and 10. The super-engineer appears in Ch 1, 2, 3 ("accidental diminisher"), and 4 ("default promotion"). Ruinous empathy is in Ch 6 and 9, Wiseman in Ch 3, 5, and 10, and the calendar audit in Ch 1–4. Each repeat is short, but together they make Part I feel circular. Rule: one owner chapter per idea (add these to the CLAUDE.md ownership map: manager-effect evidence = Ch 1, staff mandate = Ch 4, delegation levels = Ch 2). Elsewhere, one sentence plus a link.
- **The same Northstar figures come back.** 126 of 180 pages and 54 of 64 after-hours pages (Ch 3, 6, 10), the 15-week backfill and 2.3 engineer-years (Ch 3, 7, 8, 10), and Ines's eight hours a week (Ch 3, 10). Repetition helps readers who jump around, but each chapter re-quotes several figures where one would do. Rule: quote at most one baseline figure per point and link the baseline page for the rest.
- **"Northstar so far" boxes are placed inconsistently and the timeline jumps.** The box sits in the Mental Model (Ch 3), the Engineering Organization Example (Ch 2, 4, 8, 9, 10), or the Case Study (Ch 1, 5, 6, 7). The "now" runs: before week 1, then weeks 1–2, before week 1, week 1, week 19, before week 1, 12 months before, week 27, week 14, week 20. Rule: put the box at the top of the Case Study only, always state the week, always link timeline and cast. Where a chapter's case predates an earlier chapter's case, say so in the box.
- **Research paragraphs stack study details before the takeaway.** Ch 6's conflict section, Ch 7's validity section, Ch 8's five bias bullets (08:130–143), and Ch 10's turnover section give sample sizes, populations, and caveats first. Rule: open each research subsection with a one-sentence takeaway in plain words, then the evidence, with populations in parentheses.
- **Inconsistent terminology.** "Pull request" (Ch 4, 5, 7) and "merge request" (Ch 2, 6, 8, 9) name the same thing; Northstar uses GitLab, so choose "merge request" (or define once and stick to it). Calendar "leverage" (Ch 1: leveraged/necessary/substitutable) and Ch 3's leverage levels share a word for different scales.
- **Exercises sometimes need facts only the solution has** (Ch 1 F3; Ch 10 F1/F2 on decision paths; Ch 9 A2's standing rule). Rule: before publishing, check each exercise's premise against the chapter text.

## What works well (keep)
- Every chapter opens with a one-sentence **Mental model** blockquote and a diagram of the mechanism. Ch 2's two feedback loops, Ch 5's healthy and decaying channel, and Ch 10's pressure/event/alternative/binding model are the clearest in the scope.
- **Ch 6's annotated sample conversation** (06:242–283) shows preparation, counter-attack, withdrawal, and pause/resume in practice, with italic commentary. It is the single most useful teaching passage in these chapters. Consider the same device in Ch 9 (the first underperformance conversation) and Ch 10 (a stay interview).
- **Ch 9's diagnosis order** (expectation, system, skill, will, role fit), ranked by cost, together with the "third party could check" rule for expectations.
- **Ch 5's "news rate"** as the measure of a one-on-one, and the decay-signals table with "how it misleads".
- **Ch 7's honesty about the evidence**: the 2022 revision, no validity studies for software-specific formats, and the "what .42 buys" translation (move it earlier; see P4).
- **Ch 8's five-part "not yet"** and its three-ground pay test; the Sunil compression example makes compa-ratio concrete.
- **Ch 10's counter-offer cost table**, with "what the table does not show" (collective turnover).
- **Ch 1's "Northstar in one paragraph" box** with glossary links is a model orientation for a first-time reader. Every chapter's "Northstar so far" box could follow it (plus a cast link).
- Evidence labels are used consistently and honestly, and the Trade-offs sections state transition costs, as the voice rules require.
- Exercises are real situations with competing constraints, mostly solvable from the text, and the solutions name the weak answer.
