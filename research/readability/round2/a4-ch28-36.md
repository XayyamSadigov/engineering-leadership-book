- What the reader experiences: Chapter 33's Failure Modes cites "29 of 86 dependency links, Chapter 16" at line 331. The term is defined only in the Case Study, 90 lines later (line 422). ## Patterns across your scope
- **Inside-baseball cross-references in cases**: present in every chapter ("Chapter 14's open question", "the week 9 Sev1", "Bram's expectations plan (Chapter 9)", "the 30-day patch-level fix… in Chapter 26"). Book-wide rule: a case may point to another chapter only after stating the fact it needs, in one clause, so the pointer is optional reading.
- **Unresolved running threads**: core/permissions ownership, Customer Experience structure (Chapter 14) and search-svc ownership are mentioned as "open" in five or more chapters each. Rule: each thread gets one canonical line on the timeline page. Chapters that do not advance the thread do not mention it.
- **Research paragraphs that stack statistics** (Ch 30:117–137, Ch 32:104–119, Ch 34:84–91). Rule: open each evidence paragraph with the claim it supports, keep at most two numbers in prose, and put the rest in a small table or cut them.
- **Wide tables with sentence-length cells** (Ch 30 risk register, Ch 31:451, Ch 35:442 and 449–451, Ch 36:454 and 494–505). Rule: at most five columns, and cells of about 12 words or fewer; detail goes in a note under the table, consistent with the 390 px rule already applied to diagrams.
- **"Northstar so far" boxes work but omit who is who.** Rule: every box links the cast page as well as the timeline.
- **Exercise data scattered across pages**: Ch 34 F1 expects facts (Farah's technical share, Elena's practices, Daniel's status one-on-ones) found only in baseline pages or other chapters. Rule: an exercise either cites the facts it needs or names the exact page section.

## What works well (keep)
- Every chapter states its mental model early in a blockquote and builds on it. Chapter 29's critic/hero loop and Chapter 36's "large batch" framing are especially easy to retell.
- Evidence labels are honest and specific. Examples: the Prime Video correction (Ch 31:119–131), "Label the transfer" for team cognitive load (Ch 33:177), the refusal to use Dunbar or consulting-firm failure rates (Ch 33:196, Ch 36:98).
- Decision tables and flowcharts are concrete and phone-readable. Examples: Ch 28's decision-rights table with the "agreement right", Ch 31's boundary-strength table, Ch 33's signal-to-move table, Ch 35's band table.
- Every metric comes with how it is gamed (Ch 29 measures table, Ch 32 adoption table), as the voice rules require.
- Ch 36's transition-cost arithmetic and the full-versus-smaller-option payback table (lines 272–297) are the clearest worked example in scope. They also make Ch 34's "recount" and Ch 35's "cheaper answer first" concrete.
- Ch 31's five-step shared-database split and Ch 28's "recovered records" subsection answer the question "so what do I do on Monday?" directly.
- The cases end with open decisions and real tensions (Victor's hallway remark, Ines's retention risk, Mateo's uncovered seat), and the exercises build on them well.
