## Top problems (most impact on the reader first, at most 15)

### P1 (high) docs/parts/15-capstone/60-capstone-northstar.md:162 — Seat-count alternatives crammed into one table cell; "a fourth seat at 77" is opaque
- What the reader experiences: Stages 7 and 8 and X1 all require an envelope "at 80, 79, and 78 engineers, with the line that absorbs a fourth seat at 77" (lines 204–205, 235, 392, 520). The only explanation is a single cell listing proposal, Alternatives B, C, D, and the VP seat. A reader who has not read Ch 36 and Ch 47 cannot tell where 77–80 comes from, and "fourth seat" reads as a fourth engineer rather than a fourth seat count. This is a core deliverable, and the reader cannot start it with confidence.
- Example: "Alternative C (Orders also split) has 78, or 77 with a VP. Alternative D (two managers of managers) has 77."
- Fix: Replace the cell with a five-row table (option, what changes, engineer seats, with VP). Define "absorbing line" once ("the allocation line that gives up an FTE when the seat count drops"). Reword the requirement everywhere as "at 80, 79, and 78, and name the line that gives up capacity if the count falls to 77."

### P2 (high) docs/parts/15-capstone/60-capstone-northstar.md:363-377 — The facts packet asks the reader to self-police pages they must read
- What the reader experiences: Ten rows (A–J), about 25 anchor links, and a rule that stages 1–6 may use rows A and B only. Row C says the baseline page itself (row B) contains "later-dated entries" that stay closed until the hinge. So the stage-1 reader has to read the baseline page while skipping certain sections of it from memory. Hindsight leakage is listed as a failure mode (line 277), which makes it very easy to fail by accident.
- Example: "Later-dated entries on the baseline page: staff engineers' time (weeks 12–13), Orders knowledge (week 14), the Billing ledger knowledge map, ..."
- Fix: Mark each later-dated section on the baseline page itself with a visible "Dated week N: capstone hinge onward" admonition, so the reader sees the rule where it applies. In the chapter, collapse rows E–I into one "CTO stages (7–10)" row with a single exception: the week-49 material is open to stages 8 and 10 only.

### P3 (high) docs/parts/13-advanced-problems/59-transformation-under-constraint-and-scaling-without-bureaucracy.md:162-192 — "Scaling without bureaucracy" is thin, out of order, and never exercised
- What the reader experiences: Half the title gets four bullets. The section sits before the load test it depends on ("built on the load test in the next section", line 168). The worked example, the enterprise application, the case study, and every exercise are about cuts and removal, none about growth. Objective 5 (designing how a growing organization adds process) is stated, not practiced. The intro says the chapter "ends the argument with growth", but the growth material sits in the middle.
- Example: "The four practices below are **this book's recommendation**, built on the load test in the next section; no research tests them as a package."
- Fix: Move "Adding process as you grow" after "Process that carries load, process that carries history". Add a 300–500-word worked example (for example Halvard going from 400 to 500 engineers: process budget in engineer-weeks, two rules added with sunsets, one lapsing at review). Add one Applied exercise on writing the load, signal, and sunset for a proposed new rule.

### P4 (medium) docs/parts/13-advanced-problems/57-crises-executive-disagreement-and-resistance.md:557-571 — Pushback table written in Northstar shorthand

A reader who skipped Ch 43 to 55 can read the Form and Reading columns but not "What it carries", which is where the lesson is.

### P7 (medium) docs/parts/13-advanced-problems/58-layoffs-reductions-and-mergers.md:553 — Reference to an "authorship approach described above" that was never described

## Patterns across your scope
- **Week-number shorthand in case studies**: worst in Ch 57's case study (weeks 14, 40, 41, 42, 47, 48, 49 in one section), Ch 59's inventory table (weeks 3, 7, 9, 10, 11, 34, 39), and Ch 60 throughout. Recommended book-wide rule: any week reference in Part XIII or later carries a three-to-six-word reminder of the event ("week 9, the double-applied credit notes Sev1"), and each case-study banner links the timeline *and* the cast.
- **Northstar reader aids are linked unevenly**: Ch 56, 57, and 59 link the timeline or the quarter-4 briefing but never `cast.md`, even though Ch 57's case study and Ch 56's A3 introduce five or more executives at once. Ch 58's Northstar exercise X1 (the $16.73M FY2 draft and the "87-seat rule") has no Northstar link at all. Rule: every chapter whose case or exercises name three or more Northstar people links cast.md in its banner, and every Northstar exercise in a mostly Halvard chapter links the overview.
- **Exercises that depend on facts outside the chapter**: Ch 56 F1 and F3, Ch 58 X1, Ch 59 A3, Ch 60 F2. Rule: when an exercise needs facts the chapter does not state, name the page and section in the exercise.
- **Undefined HR, legal, and finance terms in otherwise careful prose**: "requisitions" (58:261), "severance waiver" and "group waiver of age claims" (58:143, 159), "vesting" (58:177), "close" and "deal model" (58:349, 497), "period close" (57:565), "material" incident (57:85), "attestation" (59:420), "merit increases" and "backfilling" (59:95–96), "stay conversations" (56:291, while the glossary has "stay interview"). The glossary covers "fully loaded cost", "skip-level", and "calibration", but the chapters do not link them. Rule: link the glossary at first use in each chapter, and add "run-rate versus in-year", "requisition", "period close", and "incident bridge" to the glossary.
- **Number-heavy example tables**: Ch 56 line 263–272, Ch 58 line 300–306 and 457–459, Ch 59 line 413–444, Ch 60 line 162–197. Each is individually correct, but they stack derivations inside cells. Rule: one number per cell where possible. Put the derivations in an "Assumptions" line under the table and state the lesson before the table.
- **Halvard is a good device**: Ch 56, 57, and 58 introduce Halvard groups that stand alone, so the reader does not need earlier chapters. Keep this pattern; Northstar load is lowest wherever Halvard carries the worked example.

## Missing or thin content
- **Scaling without bureaucracy (Ch 59)**: no worked example, no exercise, and the section sits before the load test it depends on. Add a Halvard growth example and an Applied exercise. Rough size: 500–800 words.
- **Mergers and the acquired side (Ch 58)**: mergers of peer organizations, retention of key acquired engineers, the acquired director's day-one message, and comparing the two ladders. Add a subsection after "Integrating an acquired team". Rough size: 500–700 words.
- **Resistance worked example with numbers (Ch 57)**: the crisis topic has a full Halvard example, but the disagreement and resistance topics are shown only in the Northstar case, in shorthand. A short Halvard or generic example of diagnosing one resisted change (objections collected, readings assigned, one design change made) would make the resistance diagnostic usable. Rough size: 300–400 words.
- **The leader's own position in a recovery (Ch 56)**: the chapter covers keeping or replacing inherited leaders but not what the new leader does if their own mandate is unachievable. The CTO section touches this in one sentence (line 450–451). A short paragraph on renegotiating the mandate at day 30 would fit the "one promise" output. Rough size: 150 words.
- **Capstone exemplar (Ch 60)**: no sample stage deliverable (even one page of stage 1 counting rules) shows what "good enough" looks like. Northstar's record is called "one worked answer", but it is spread across many pages. A one-page example of stage 1's trust table and counting rules would anchor the whole assignment. Rough size: 1 page, in the solutions file.
- **Unexplained references (Ch 59)**: Lee and Edmondson, Harter, and Rajan and Wulf appear only in the reference list ("other work on spans ... see the references", line 234), so the reader cannot tell what they add. Add one line each or drop them.

## What works well (keep)
- Ch 56's three-loop mental model (capability, trust, people) and its diagram. The model is stated early and the rest of the chapter uses it ("name the strongest loop").
- Ch 56's triage outputs table, especially "defensible even if the rest of the diagnosis is wrong", and the "What the triage missed" passage. That passage honestly applies the chapter's rule against the director's own record (the Billing ledger engineers).
- Ch 56's mapping of trust factors to stakeholders (the CFO doubts ability, sales doubts benevolence, customer success doubts integrity). It is memorable and actionable.
- Ch 57's Halvard Checkout timeline, the "true and misleading" support macro, and the quoted next-morning correction. This is the clearest teaching example in the scope.
- Ch 57's commit-or-escalate table, and the concept of a written floor set before the dispute.
- Ch 58's "decide the work before the people" sequence, with the skipped-step consequences table. Also the scripted first two minutes of the notification conversation, including the three things not to say.
- Ch 58's ownership check worked through with real counts, and the separate fairness checks beyond legal review.
- Ch 59's four-question load test, "capacity is not cash / in-year is not run-rate", and the compliance-pack failure ("the pack's load had been the meeting, not the document"). That failure is a vivid illustration of a hidden load.
- Ch 60 as an assignment: per-stage Produce / Add / Hand forward, a budget-and-finish-line table whose hours add up to the stated 75–100, a short path (45–55 hours), pass/fail portfolio checks, and a written definition of "done". The explanation of why stage 9 precedes stage 7 prevents a likely point of confusion.
- Ch 60's two-thread case study (core/permissions, ADR-0001), which shows the hand-forward failure in a way no single chapter can.
