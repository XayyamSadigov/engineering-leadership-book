## Top problems (most impact on the reader first, at most 15)

### P1 (high) 21-technical-debt.md:489 — "Renewal commitments" and "renewal teams" used everywhere, explained nowhere in Part V
- What the reader experiences: Exercises and cases repeatedly lean on "the two renewal commitments", "the two renewal teams", "behind on a renewal commitment", "one of the two renewal customers" (ch21:489–491, ch22:503 and 507, ch24:486–487 and 549, ch26:504 and 577, ch27:26 and 488–490, ch20:519). A reader who starts at Part V does not know that these are two promised features (bulk ordering, accounting integration) owned by Orders and Payments and Integrations, which is why those teams get only 10 percent prevention allocation. The glossary has `#renewal-commitments` and `#the-five-constraints`, but no chapter in scope links them.
- Example: "Amara Okafor, the CPO, has agreed to the quarter's 12 FTE prevention allocation (25 percent of Billing's capacity, 10 percent in the two renewal teams, 15 percent elsewhere)."
- Fix: At first use in each chapter, add a one-clause gloss and link: "the two [renewal commitments](../../glossary.md#renewal-commitments) (a bulk-ordering feature and an accounting integration promised to two customers worth $9M a year)". In ch27:490 say explicitly that Orders and Payments and Integrations own them.

### P2 (high) 27-quality-transformation-at-enterprise-scale.md:205 — "constraint 5 of the case" is undefined and collides with the chapter's own "constraint"
- What the reader experiences: The chapter has just taught "the constraint" in the theory-of-constraints sense (the release train). Then "constraint 5 of the case" appears with no link and no list. The reader cannot tell whether this is a fifth bottleneck or something else.
- Example: "At Northstar, constraint 5 of the case required any plan to say what the seven QA engineers would do next."
- Fix: Rewrite as "One of the [five fixed conditions](../../glossary.md#the-five-constraints) on the director's plan says that any plan changing the QA engineers' work must say what they do next." Consider renaming the canon's "constraints" to "fixed conditions" book-wide so the word "constraint" means only the bottleneck in Part V.

- Fix: In ch24, replace the table with counts by loss type ("could not order: 2; wrong money or data: 8; other: 4") and two named examples; move the full list into a `??? note`. In ch23, move the ten-incident table into the Engineering Organization Example or a collapsible note and keep only the four-row "route into production" table up front. Link the glossary's `#inc-numbers` entry once per chapter.

### P4 (medium) 20-engineering-standards-and-their-governance.md:280 — "five tier 1 minimums" contradicts "four operational minimums"
- What the reader experiences: The chapter introduces four operational minimums (line 261, table at 265–271, CTO section at 394). Then the triage arithmetic says "Add the five tier 1 minimums and the mandatory set falls … to 19". The reader stops to work out what the fifth is (presumably the auditable pipeline) and whether 14 + 5 = 19 or 14 + 4 = 18.
- Example: "Add the five tier 1 minimums and the mandatory set falls from 52 unverified rules to 19, each checked, sampled, or reported per component."
- Fix: Name the five: "the four operational minimums plus the auditable-pipeline standard". Or correct the count if the fifth is not intended.

### P5 (medium) 23-code-review-ci-cd-and-release-engineering.md:166 — A part larger than its whole

### P9 (medium) 24-reliability-slos-and-operational-readiness.md:242 — The rotation-design objective is thinly delivered

### P10 (medium) 26-security-and-software-supply-chain-quality.md:202 — Severity scale and provenance never explained; acronym load high

Replace "The abstract supports a mechanism rather than a number" with "The lesson is a mechanism, not a number". Drop SPDX and CycloneDX names or put them in a parenthesis as "two standard formats".

## Patterns across your scope
- **Repeated Northstar recap.** Every chapter re-explains Sev1 and Sev2 in a parenthesis (19:32, 21:240, 22:245, 23:22, 24:27, 25:19, 26:22, 27:23), restates $410,000 and 30 incidents, and announces "12 FTE in week 4", often in both the opening and the "Northstar so far" box. Recommended rule: the "Northstar so far" box carries the recap. Prose defines Sev1/Sev2 once per chapter, by glossary link, at first use.
- **Cross-chapter retelling of episodes** (week-9 Sev1, QA announcement in week 10, release working group, quarantine rule; see P7). Rule: one owning chapter tells each episode in full; others give one sentence, a link, and only what is new to them.
- **INC numbers as shorthand** (P3). Most uses come with a short description, which helps, but some sections carry 10 to 19 IDs at once. Rule: at most about five INC IDs per section in prose. Longer lists go in a collapsible note. Always pair an ID with a few words of description. Link `#inc-numbers` once per chapter.
- **Northstar reader aids exist but are unlinked.** No chapter in scope links `cast.md` or `quarter-4-briefing.md`. Glossary Northstar entries (`#renewal-commitments`, `#the-five-constraints`, `#inc-numbers`, `#weeks-quarters-and-years`) are never linked. People are reintroduced with their role each time, which is good. Rule: link the cast page at the first named person in each case study, and link Northstar shorthand at first use.
- **Glossary links are uneven for the part's own terms.** Ch24 does not link `#sli-slo-and-sla`, ch25 does not link `#blameless-review`, `#recurrence`, or `#prevention-point`, ch21 does not link `#technical-debt-principal-and-interest`, and ch20 does not link `#paved-road`. The chapters define these inline, so this matters only for readers jumping in mid-chapter. Rule: owning chapters define inline and also link the glossary entry once.
- **Stacked evidence qualifications in deep sections.** Honest labeling is a strength, but some paragraphs spend more words on a source's limits than on its lesson (ch21:159–180, ch24:252–258, ch25:187–208, ch26:194–198). Rule: give the lesson first in one sentence, then one sentence on the limit. Put sample details in the bibliography annotation.
- **Terminology drift**: detection, prevention, and control point (P12); "change failure rate" (Northstar) against "change fail rate" (DORA), which ch23:261–264 explains well but other chapters do not; "tier 1 minimums" counted as four or five (P4). Rule: maintain a Part V term list and lint for variants.
- **Exercises are generally solvable from the text plus the linked data files.** The exceptions lean on untaught material: ch24 A1 (rotation mechanics, P9), ch25 A1 (the commander's first minutes, mitigate-before-diagnose), and ch21 F3 (needs headcount 80, which is not stated in ch21).

## Missing or thin content
- **On-call rotation design** (ch24, Deep Conceptual Explanation, "On-call design and load"): primary/secondary, shadowing, handoff, escalation, compensation forms, spreading expert knowledge. About 300 words.
- **The first minutes of incident response** (ch25, after "Roles"): declare, assign roles, mitigate before diagnosing, set the update cadence, protect the commander from executives. A five-step list of about 150 words, which also supports exercise A1.
- **Performance, load, and other non-functional testing** (ch22): the portfolio covers unit, contract, end-to-end, migration, and exploration, but load and capacity testing appears only implicitly (INC-415 is classified "load test or canary" in ch25). Add a short subsection, about 200 words, on where load tests sit and how to budget them.
- **Vulnerability severity scoring** (ch26, "Dependencies and remediation"): what CVSS is, and why base score misleads without exploitation and reachability. About 80 words.
- **Exemption register entry example** (ch20, Enterprise Application): the fields are named, but a filled two-line example (core/permissions, auth-svc) would make it concrete. The case study has the content; a small table would do it. About 60 words.

## What works well (keep)
- The contract structure is followed cleanly. "Northstar so far" boxes give a reliable re-entry point, and every chapter says which neighbor owns which topic.
- Mental models are short and memorable, and the chapters return to them: "Debt is defined by its interest", "Deploying is moving code; releasing is exposing behavior", "Reliability is a budget", "Shut off the inflow before you drain the stock".
- Worked numbers sit in collapsible notes (ch22:276, ch23:170, ch24:291, ch25:290), so the main text keeps the conclusion and the arithmetic is available on demand. Extend this to ch21 and ch27 (P6, P14).
- The ch24 incident replays (INC-403, INC-413) turn burn-rate alerting from abstraction into something a reader can check.
- The ch23 lead-time decomposition table shows exactly what a cadence change can and cannot fix, and leads naturally to "review becomes the bottleneck".
- Every case study includes something that went wrong (the reverted Jenkins check, the frontend upgrade that consumed a whole share, night pages from low-traffic burn alerts, the slipped action before the week-9 Sev1). This makes the advice believable.
- The ch25 treatment of blameless review draws a concrete, narrow accountability boundary and is honest that outcome evidence is thin.
- The ch27 capacity model shows the dip, the payback, and a pessimistic case. It is an unusually honest model for a reader to copy.
- The ch19 and ch21 tables that normalize defects, and rank by interest over principal, show plainly how naive measures mislead.
