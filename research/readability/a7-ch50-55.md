## Top problems (most impact on the reader first, at most 15)

### P1 (high) 50:470–473, 51:450–457, 54:456–466, 55:546–553 — The same unexplained "open decisions" list in every chapter
- What the reader experiences: Every case study opens by listing "the organization proposal, the quality
  continuation, the workforce plan draft, the VP question, the CI target, and ADR-0001". None of them is
  defined in these chapters. A reader who starts Part XII, or who skipped Parts VIII–XI, does not know
  what any of them is, why it matters, or why its week number matters. Each case's tension ("what makes
  it hard") depends on that list, so the lesson goes blurry exactly where it should land.
- Example: "On your desk are the director's year-2 proposals, none decided: the organization proposal
  (decision due this week), the quality continuation (executive team in week 49), the workforce plan
  draft (week 49), the VP question (recommendation due week 48), the CI target, and ADR-0001." (51:452–455)
- Fix: Write one "Northstar at the start of quarter 4" box, about 150 words, either at the top of Part
  XII or on the first-quarter page. Give each open decision one line (what it is and when it is
  decided). Do the same for recurring tokens: ADR-0001 (integrations reading 12 ledger tables
  directly), TR-01 (Core framework out of support), auth-svc (script-deployed service with an exploited
  critical vulnerability), stage gates at weeks 56, 78, and 104. Link the box from each chapter's
  opening paragraph, and cut the per-chapter re-listing.

### P2 (high) 52:224–356, 371, 444, 572 — Finance terms used without definition in the finance chapter
- What the reader experiences: The chapter states that it teaches finance to engineers, then uses terms
  a non-finance reader cannot decode:
  - "working capital" (356)
  - "fully loaded cost" (324)
  - "controller" (225, 328, 477, 515)
  - depreciation versus amortization (320; the second is explained, the first is not)
  - "discount rate" and "annuity factor 2.487" (571–572)
  - "committed-use discount" (444)
  - "Calder Ridge Partners" (371, 616), which is not identified as the investor until Chapter 55
  - "FY−2" (360)

  The reader stalls in the best example in the Part.
- Example: "Net income 16.8, plus amortization and depreciation 2.9, plus working capital 1.0 (mostly
  the growth in deferred revenue) gives operating cash flow of 20.7." (355–356)
- Fix: Define each term at first use in one clause, or add a short "Terms" box after The Mental Model.
  For the break-even (571–573), add one sentence: "an annuity factor converts a yearly amount into
  today's money at the discount rate; Chapter 45 explains it". Present the cash-flow statement
  (355–358) as a table to match the other two statements.

## Patterns across your scope
- **Open-decision soup.** Every case study turns on four to six undecided Northstar proposals,
  identified only by name and week (all six chapters). Book-wide rule: any chapter-specific case
  study states each canon item it depends on in one clause at first use, or links to a single
  state-of-play box. No case's tension may depend on an undefined proposal.
- **Ownership pointers in the opening paragraph.** Each chapter's first paragraph lists three to five
  other chapters and what they own (50:12–15, 51:12–16, 53:13–15, 54:13–18, 55:13–16). That is
  navigation for the maintainer and inside baseball for the reader. Rule: open with the chapter's
  promise, and put "Related chapters" in one line at the end of the opening, or in a margin box.
- **"Check:" arithmetic in prose** (50:277, 50:301, 51:274, 51:494, 52:544, 55:368). Rule: show
  arithmetic in a table column or the solutions file, and state the conclusion in prose.
- **Inline citation hedging** (P14). Rule: one evidence label per claim in prose; access level in the
  references.
- **Terms first used in one chapter and defined in a later one.** SBOM is defined in 55 but used in
  53. Calder Ridge Partners is identified in 55 but used in 52. Audit committee is used in 50 and
  explained only in 53:30. Rule: define at first use within the Part. Add a Part XII glossary covering
  ARR, EBITDA, FTE, attestation, SOC 2, CISO, audit committee, data room, appetite, and accepter.
- **Inconsistent names for one thing.** "SOC 2 report" / "independent attestation" / "assurance report"
  (50:442, 53:239–246, 53:282, 53:325). "Quality continuation" / "continuation proposal" / "stage 2"
  (51, 52, 53, 55). Rule: pick one term per concept for the Part and gloss alternates once.
- **The same CTO scaffolding repeated per chapter.** Every chapter has a Halvard paragraph in its CTO
  Perspective and an Enterprise Application that largely repeats its Playbook (50, 51, 53, 54). Rule:
  if Enterprise Application has nothing that the Playbook lacks, cut it to the 50–150-engineer
  specifics (staffing, who owns what) and drop the restated steps.

## Missing or thin content
- **Ch 54, AI in the product.** Inference cost and gross margin, provider dependence and change
  management, and AI regulation or contractual AI clauses are absent or one sentence each. Add a
  ~300-word subsection in Deep Explanation, or state them as gaps.
- **Ch 54, data strategy beyond readiness.** There is nothing on funding a data platform, data
  ownership as an ongoing operating model, or data products. Either add ~250 words to the CTO
  Perspective, or narrow the title and objectives.
- **Ch 53, third-party and vendor risk.** X3 asks how vendor risk is represented at Halvard, but the
  chapter only mentions vendors in the register's scope (88) and in "transfer". Add a ~150-word
  paragraph on vendor and supplier risk in the register (contract, concentration, evidence from the
  vendor).
- **Ch 53, business continuity and disaster recovery, and cyber insurance.** Continuity appears only as
  ER-09 restore tests. Insurance appears only as a transfer example. A reader expecting "risk strategy"
  will look for both. Add one paragraph each, or list them as a stated gap like privacy (255–257).
- **Ch 52, glossary-level finance definitions** (P2). Working capital, depreciation, fully loaded cost,
  controller, discount rate, and annuity factor. Roughly 150–200 words.
- **Ch 51 A2, Ch 55 F3, Ch 53 F1, Ch 52 F2, Ch 54 A3: facts needed to solve the exercises** (P7). One
  line per canon item inside the exercise or the Example.
- **Ch 50, the CTO's first 90 days or the transition into the seat.** It is cited (Larson, 2020) but
  not taught. A reader newly promoted to CTO would expect a short sequence. Consider ~200 words in the
  Playbook, or a pointer if another chapter owns it.

## What works well (keep)
- **Ch 50.** The mental model ("the CTO's job is the decisions only the top technologist can take; the
  calendar is the evidence"), the decision-rights table by role (150–158), the seven-item "only the
  CTO" list, and the keep/give/stop table with named receivers are concrete and reusable.
- **Ch 51.** The four bins (excellent / adequate / buy / not doing), "horizons as kinds of bet, not
  years", the four-field option record (assumption, probe, decision point, kill criterion), and the
  seven coherence tests. The case's executive pushback (Amara splitting the ordering line; Greg's
  objection kept on the record) shows the strategy saying no, which most books only assert.
- **Ch 52.** The three-lens mental model with its flowchart and table (53–86). The full Northstar
  income statement and balance sheet with the "where the $20.3M is" reconciliation. The "if everything
  were expensed" table (363–368). The prevention-versus-feature income-statement trap (376–380). This is
  the clearest finance teaching for engineers in the book.
- **Ch 53.** "A risk register is a decision record, not an inventory", the owner/accepter split, appetite
  written in indicator units, and the example where one amber "Cyber" line becomes nine owned risks,
  with the highest one not a security risk.
- **Ch 54.** The amplifier model. The study table with setting, outcome, and result side by side
  (200–206) and the four readings under it. Decomposing "AI order checking" into causes, 59 percent of
  which need no AI (286–298). Pricing the CFO's backfill question with stated assumptions.
- **Ch 55.** The exposure translation table (341–348), the directors'-concerns table (557–564), the four
  destinations of a diligence finding with the Yahoo/Verizon illustration, and the four extra
  decision-record fields (what we would have to believe, dissent, reversal triggers, review date).
- **Across the Part.** Every case ends open with "the reader decides", the exercises are realistic,
  and the solutions show criteria and the weak answer. Keep that structure.
