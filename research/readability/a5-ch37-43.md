## Top problems (most impact on the reader first, at most 15)

### P1 (high) 41-managing-and-developing-engineering-managers.md:354 — Case evidence is a chain of cross-references
- What the reader experiences: each manager's evidence paragraph (lines 354–398) cites 3 to 6 earlier chapters for facts the reader is supposed to remember: "(Chapter 2)", "(Chapter 21)", "(Chapter 4)", "(Chapter 27)", "(Chapter 16)", "(Chapter 22)", "(Chapter 30)", "(Chapters 19, 20, 22)", "(Chapter 10)". A reader who did not read Parts II to VII in order can't check any rating, and the lesson (ratings must trace to dated evidence) disappears under the bookkeeping.
- Example: "Billing reserved weeks 1 to 3 for the ledger export (Chapter 16), reviewed contract expectations for meaning (Chapter 22), and Marcus raised the tax-svc risk himself (Chapter 30): leverage is Solid."
- Fix: work two managers in full (Marcus and Daniel are the most instructive) as evidence tables with columns dimension / dated evidence item / source / rating, written so they stand alone without the chapter references. Cut the other four to one line each. Put the chapter pointers in one footnote per manager.

### P2 (high) 41-managing-and-developing-engineering-managers.md:94 — The rating rule contradicts the case
- What the reader experiences: line 94 says "Rate Not yet only against an expectation the manager knew", and the decision flow (line 211) sends "Expectation stated? No" to "State it; re-rate later". The case table (lines 343–344) then rates Daniel and Farah "Not yet (unset)", and line 349 admits three more Not yet cells rest on facts from before the window. A careful reader concludes either that the rule is not meant seriously or that they have misunderstood it.
- Example: "Where none was stated, the gap is the director's too, and it may inform development but not pay this cycle."
- Fix: replace "Not yet (unset)" with a fifth visible state such as "Expectation not set" (no rating), or show the director re-rating those cells as the rule requires. Rewrite "not pay this cycle" as "it may shape the development plan but must not affect this cycle's pay or performance rating".

### P3 (high) 38-scorecards-quarterly-planning-and-capacity-allocation.md:53 — Planning vocabulary used before it is defined
- What the reader experiences: the mental model (line 53) and the flow diagram (line 157) rely on "cautious case" and "planned case", which are defined only by example at line 213. The worked example mixes FTE (never expanded in the chapter), FTE-quarters, and engineer-weeks across lines 205–242, so a reader can't tell whether 35.6 is people or work.
- Example: "About 35.6 FTE-quarters of estimated effort, from the top of the ranked list, each cross-team item with a lead team. The next 3.8 are targets that start only if the planned case holds."
- Fix: at the start of the Deep Conceptual Explanation, add a short glossary box: FTE (one full-time engineer), FTE-quarter (one engineer for 13 weeks = 13 engineer-weeks), planned case (capacity if unplanned work falls as projected), cautious case (capacity if it stays at last quarter's level), growth allowance, target band. Then use one unit (FTE-quarters) in the example, with engineer-weeks only in parentheses.

### P4 (high) 37-cadences-and-leadership-meetings.md:232 — Calendar arithmetic buries the audit finding
- What the reader experiences: the 14-row hours table (215–230) is followed by a paragraph that reconciles averages across weeks 14–19 and week 16 onward. Later, lines 426–433 list every manager's hours as decimals. The real lesson, "hours were not the main finding" (line 239), arrives after the reader is worn out.
- Example: "Skip-levels started in week 16, so over weeks 14 to 19 they averaged 1.33 hours (4 of 6 weeks) and the recurring total averaged 15.6; the comparison with quarter 3 uses the rate from week 16 (none in weeks 1 to 15)."
- Fix: lead with the topic-by-forum matrix (lines 241–248), which is the chapter's signature tool. Shrink the hours table to five grouped rows (operational review, other weekly forums, one-on-ones and skip-levels, monthly, quarterly). Delete lines 232–235. Replace the decimals in lines 426–433 with one sentence: "Managers' forum hours rise slightly, from about 4.1 to 4.6 a week; the redesign changes what they spend it on, not how much."

### P5 (high) 43-leading-change-and-director-failure-patterns.md:182 — "Escalation" means opposite things in Ch 39 and Ch 43
- What the reader experiences: Ch 39 teaches escalation as a healthy mechanism (joint case, clock, log). Three chapters later, "Escalation" is the name of a director failure pattern (line 182, also lines 41 and 202). A reader skimming the patterns table reads it as "escalating disputes is bad".
- Example: "| Escalation | Defending a failing initiative because it is yours |"
- Fix: rename the pattern "Escalation of commitment" (or "Doubling down") everywhere in Ch 43, including the diagram text at line 202, the self-assessment table at line 437, and the takeaways. Book-wide rule: bare "escalation" always means moving a decision upward.

### P6 (medium) 42-cross-functional-dependencies-portfolio-and-influence.md:63 — "Coalition" carries three meanings across Ch 42–43

The chapter has to spend a bullet (198–200) undoing its own word choice.

### P9 (medium) 39-reviews-decision-logs-escalation-and-executive-reporting.md:229 — The review-cost table has a weak payoff

## Patterns across your scope
- **Reconciliation arithmetic in prose** ("Check: ...", "matching the canon", "match Chapter 37's 9.5 hours"): Ch 38:233, Ch 39:237, Ch 40:398–399, Ch 41:192 and 347, Ch 42:231, Ch 43:391. These lines serve the canon review, not the reader. Book-wide rule: move consistency checks to the solutions or an HTML comment; show a sum only when the total is the point.
- **Inside-baseball week numbers and earlier-chapter citations used as evidence**: worst in Ch 41's case and Ch 40's period table, present in Ch 37 and Ch 39. Rule: any fact from an earlier chapter is restated in one self-contained clause; the chapter reference is optional and goes at the end.
- **The same idea taught twice**: bad news traveling upward appears in Ch 39:104–134 (Smith and Keil, mum effect) and again in Ch 40:96–117 (Williamson, Milliken) with no cross-link. The skip-level rule is stated in Ch 40:156–159, restated in Ch 41:121–126, and again in Ch 41's playbook. The corridor or backchannel decision appears in Ch 37:333, Ch 39:194, and Ch 39:313. Grove's "managerial output" is introduced in Ch 39:83 by pointing forward to Ch 40. Rule: one chapter teaches the idea; the others give a one-line reminder and a link. Specifically, Ch 40 should link to Ch 39's "Why bad news travels slowly" rather than rebuild it.
- **Forward references**: Ch 39:83 and 134 send readers to Ch 40 for basics Ch 39 needs; Ch 43 references Ch 57 "will cover". Rule: avoid forward references for core concepts. Define the concept in a sentence where it is first used.
- **Inconsistent names for the same forum**: "managers' meeting", "leadership team meeting", "managers' weekly forum", "weekly meeting with the managers" (Ch 41:131), and "the director's weekly meeting with the managers" all refer to overlapping things across Chapters 37, 41, and 43. Likewise "monthly business review", "monthly delivery review", and "portfolio forum" (Ch 42:321 places it inside the business review). Rule: after Ch 37, use only "leadership team meeting" and "monthly business review", with the portfolio forum explicitly a section of the latter.
- **Decimal hours**: 0.31, 0.375, 1.125, 0.23, 10.375 (Ch 37). Rule: round to the nearest quarter hour or use minutes; precision invites checking rather than reading.
- **What works structurally**: every chapter has a single-sentence mental model and a diagram of it in the first screen. Keep this convention.

Add a five-line charter example and a five-field weekly update (what changed, risks, decisions needed, asks, metrics link), about 150 words.
- **Ch 37: preparation cost is promised but not modeled.** Objective 4 and the Mankins discussion promise it, but no example estimates preparation hours. Add two sentences to the case estimating preparation for the current versus proposed operational review.
- **Ch 38: how capacity by category is measured.** "Time sampling" appears (lines 322, 477) without explanation or a link to where Ch 16 or Ch 27 explains it. Add a two-sentence explanation and a link.
- **Ch 38: one worked, paired OKR.** F3 and X1 ask for them; the chapter gives only rules and one bad example. Add one good objective with two key results and their pairs, about 80 words.
- **Ch 39: a worked joint escalation case.** A1 asks the reader to write the template; the chapter describes it only in a sentence (line 165). Add a five-field template (question, options, each side's recommendation, cost of delay, deadline), about 60 words.
- **Ch 40: managing staff engineers as direct reports.** Mentioned (line 309) but never treated, even though the director has three. One paragraph, or a pointer to Ch 29.
- **Ch 41: removing or moving a manager who is not working out.** The "Role conversation" branch points to Ch 9, but the director-specific steps (team communication, interim cover, a return-to-IC path) are absent. 200–300 words in Decision Framework or Enterprise Application.
- **Ch 41: hiring managers from outside and onboarding first-time managers.** Only one line (line 286). A short subsection, about 200 words.
- **Ch 42: what to do when the CPO and director disagree on rank.** The rule "director owns arithmetic, cost-bearers own the trade-off" (line 267) leaves the deadlock case open. One paragraph.
- **Ch 43: communicating a change after the coalition has shaped it.** The chapter warns against announcement but never says what a good launch message looks like. About 150 words, possibly reusing Ch 37's "what each member will tell their team".
